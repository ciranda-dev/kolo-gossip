# Limits

## Policy

- No unnecessary limits. The library does not cap the size of a topic, the number of authors, the message rate or the retention of history. The application's validator decides who may post and how often, and the pins decide how long history is kept.
- Only protective bounds. A bound exists only to stop a hostile or broken peer from making memory, CPU or bandwidth grow without limit.
- Every bound is a `Config` field, and a zero field takes the default below. A negative or inconsistent value is `ErrInvalidConfig`. To raise a bound, set its field: the Source column says what a larger value costs.
- No count of peers, topics or rooms is a limit of this library by default: `MaxPeers` and `MaxTopics` are 0, no cap, and a positive value is a cap the application chose (`ErrLimit`). Connections are bounded by the ones `ServeConn` is given, which kolo-swarm bounds with its place budget; topics are only the rooms the application joins.
- Every default has a written source: the GossipSub specification, the value deployed implementations of it ship, a value derived from those or from another Kolo library's limit, or a measurement with our simulator.

Every length is checked against its limit before anything is allocated for it. A frame body then grows only as its bytes arrive, starting at 4 KiB, so a peer that announces a large frame and sends little of it holds little memory, and only until it stalls for `FrameTimeout`. A decoded control frame allocates its lists from counts already bounded by the bytes of the frame.

## Scoring

`DefaultScore` and `DefaultTopicScore` hold the score parameters our simulator found (thresholds Gossip -10, Publish -20, Graylist -80, OpportunisticGraft 3; three invalid messages bring a peer to the graylist threshold and a fourth below it; ten behaviour offences are free; the penalties of a connected peer decay to nothing in 10 minutes; P3 and P3b off). The score of a peer that disconnects is kept unchanged, without decay, for `RetainScore` (10 minutes), as the specification asks, so reconnecting does not clear a penalty; `RetainScore` must be positive. The specification publishes no values, so these are measured. The peer exchange of a received PRUNE is dialed only from a peer whose score is at least `Thresholds.AcceptPX`, 12 by default: one above `TopicScoreCap` (11), so only a peer the application vouches for through its application score (P5) qualifies; it must be at least 0, and 0 dials no peer exchange at all, as a hand-built `Thresholds` has (GossipSub v1.1 asks for a positive threshold). With scoring off no peer exchange is dialed, since nothing vouches for the sender. `Config.NoScore` turns scoring off; `TopicOptions.NoScore` leaves one topic unscored, and then invalid messages in it cost nothing. A zero `ScoreParams` or `TopicScoreParams` is refused.

A peer that falls below the graylist threshold has every frame dropped; its `ServeConn` ends with `ErrGraylisted`, checked after each of its frames and at every heartbeat, its connection is closed, and `Allow` refuses its key until its score recovers or its retained score expires. IP colocation (P6) counts peers by the IPv4 address of their connection; a relayed connection exposes no address (kolo-relay's `Conn` has no `RemoteAddr`), so relayed peers are left out of P6 and are bounded by the relay's and kolo-swarm's own limits instead.

## Connections and rooms

| Field | Default | Protects | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PeerQueue` | 32 control frames per peer | Memory, slow peers | The per-peer outbound queue of deployed implementations. Messages are bounded by `PeerQueueBytes` alone: the answer to one IWANT is one frame per message there and can hold thousands |
| `PeerQueueBytes` | the largest of 1 MiB, `MaxMessageSize` and `MaxControlSize` | Memory, slow peers | 1 MiB per peer, raised to hold one largest frame |
| `RoomQueue` | 32 messages | Memory, slow readers | The subscription buffer of deployed implementations |
| `FrameTimeout` | 30 s without a byte | Memory held by a stalled frame or stream header; a place held by a peer that opens no stream | kolo-transport's `IdleTimeout`: a peer silent that long is gone. It bounds a stall, not a slow frame: a frame that keeps arriving is read whole. Waiting between frames has no limit. A peer that opens neither of its streams within it is treated as stalled |
| `RetryInterval` | `HeartbeatInterval` (1 s) | CPU of validators asked to run again | The router's finest timer, so the default retries once a heartbeat: a message the validator answered `Retry` is validated again at the first heartbeat at least `RetryInterval` after its last `Retry`, or at once on `Room.Revalidate`. A held message counts in `MaxValidating`, `MaxValidatingBytes` and its sender's share until it is decided or reaches `SeenTTL`, so it costs at most `SeenTTL` / `RetryInterval` validator runs (120 runs in all, the first included, at the defaults in `TestRetryEndsAtSeenTTL`) and at most `MaxValidating` runs per interval for the node, each charged to the sender's reader bucket like a first validation. To raise the interval, set it; an application that calls `Room.Revalidate` itself can set it to a long time |
| `ValidateTimeout` | `SeenTTL` (2 min), at most `SeenTTL` | A slow validator | The longest a message is kept in the seen window. A validator still running past its timeout keeps its message charged to the share of the peer that sent it until it returns, and counts in `Stats.StuckValidators` meanwhile; a verdict after `SeenTTL` is an Ignore. Each validation has its own number and a verdict applies only to it. When the router drops a message whose validator runs (past the departed share, on `Leave`), it cancels the validator's context and keeps the validation in `MaxValidating` and `MaxValidatingBytes` until the validator returns, counted in `Stats.StuckValidators` while it runs on in a room still joined; `Close` cancels every validator and never waits for one that ignores its context |

- Messages held for a `Retry` stay charged to their sender's `MaxPeerValidating` share and to the global bounds, so a peer cannot hold more of them than it could hold messages being validated; a held message reaches `SeenTTL` and is dropped. `RouterStats.Validating` includes them.
- `TopicOptions.AllowPeer` keeps one 32-byte topic id for each (peer, topic) pair it refused, while the peer is connected. Only our own answers create the entries, so the memory is at most 32 bytes times the connected peers times the topics that have an `AllowPeer`, and no peer can raise it.
- A full peer queue drops the frame and counts it in `RouterStats.SendDropped`; GRAFT, PRUNE and subscriptions are kept and sent with the next control frame. Frames being written still count, so a peer that never reads holds at most the bound. The other peers are not affected.
- Every control frame but one of IHAVE alone goes before other control frames, so a subscription, GRAFT or PRUNE is never overtaken by a later one and a request never waits behind gossip, and messages travel on their own stream, so an IDONTWANT never waits behind a large message. IDONTWANT ids queued while the writer is busy join the last IDONTWANT frame, up to `MaxIDontWantLength` ids each.
- Each of a peer's two streams has one frame in the router at a time: the reader waits for the router before it reads the next frame, so QUIC flow control slows that peer alone.
- A full room queue drops the message for that room only, counted in `RoomStats.Dropped`; the node has already forwarded it.
- A peer may open one control and one message stream. A third stream, a second stream of one kind, an unknown version or kind, a frame over its limit, a malformed control frame, a control frame of more than `MaxCertWants` certificates or more than 32 GRAFT or PRUNE entries closes the connection with code `0x6b67`. The 32 is a frame rule, the receiver's bound of every released version (it bounds the lock time of one frame, the costliest measured row being a PRUNE of 16 topics into full backoff tables), not a limit on topics: 50 topics take two frames. A frame of subscriptions has no entry cap, only `MaxControlSize` (about 15,887 entries), and announcing many topics is no violation: the frame is sorted and merged once, charged by its entries and the lists it moves, and what does not fit `MaxPeerTopics` is dropped. The control frames we send never exceed these bounds: a frame carries at most `MaxCertWants` certificates and 32 GRAFT and 32 PRUNE entries; further answers, and PRUNEs kept after a full queue, go in further frames.
- The id and the signature of a new message are computed by the peer's own stream reader, outside the router lock, and charged to its reader bucket by the message's size (the signature check hashes all of it); the router never checks a signature itself on that path. An author's chain is checked with two kolo-id checks, now and an hour ahead (or the second before the device's expiry): when both pass, the result is kept until that second, since kolo-id's answer changes with time only when a link expires; when the later one fails, a link expires within the hour and the result is kept for its second only. A failure is kept until the revocation list changes. Results are found by the objects the roster returns, or by the hash of their encodings; only first objects are kept. A CERT frame's chain checks are paid before they run, within a budget of what is left in its sender's lock bucket (0 when it is in debt) plus one chain check per certificate of the frame (at most `MaxCertWants`), so reading the frame never makes us drop honest answers; once the budget is spent, its remaining requests end without a penalty.
- Every frame is charged to its peer in work units (10 ns of a core each) where the work happens: the call, each lookup, allocation, encoding and queued frame, each list entry looked at, whether past a cap or not, the bytes decoded or hashed, each signature and chain check. Units are rounded up from benchmarks, and a unit audit test fails if a handler does work it does not charge to the peer that sent the frame. Application code under the lock (a roster lookup, the open mode, the application score) is timed with the monotonic clock and charged by its time, at least 20 units a call; the validator's time is not charged. Each peer has two buckets, one under the lock and one on its reader, refilled continuously. When one is empty, the peer's streams are not read until it is positive again: QUIC flow control slows that peer alone, and its score does not change. A peer that waits drops its IWANT promises and chain requests without a penalty, and they are asked of other peers at once.
- An answer to our own IWANT that brings the message gives the asked peer back the work it cost; no other peer gets anything back. An id is asked of at most two peers at once, and one peer is asked at most the smaller of `MaxIHaveLength` and `DesignRate` ids at once. Up to two more peers that announce it are kept, and one peer waits to be asked for at most as many ids as it may be asked for (1,666): while the id stays missing, one of them is asked each heartbeat, and a place freed by a peer that waits or leaves is filled at the next heartbeat. Each peer keeps an index of the ids it is asked for or waits for, so a peer that waits or leaves frees its places at a cost that depends on its own asks alone. A new ask is charged when it is made for the heartbeat visits it will get: 3 x (ceil(`IWantFollowupTime` / `HeartbeatInterval`) + 3) lookups, 18 lookups or 36 units at the defaults. IWANT answers are counted in the peer, per id, at most `GossipRetransmission` each and at most `MaxCacheMessages` ids, cleared every `HistoryLength` heartbeats and dropped when the peer leaves; a reconnect starts a new count, which gives a key nothing a fresh key does not get.
- A chain request unanswered for a heartbeat is joined by one to the next peer that sent a copy, every heartbeat; a request whose peer waits or leaves ends at once and the next peer is asked.
- `AddPeer` refuses a peer only when a `MaxPeers` the application set is reached (`ErrLimit`); churn never refuses one. A key starts with what the newcomer pool gives (at most one second of the pool's share, `LockWork` / 64 and `PeerWork` / 64, the pool refilling at that one share for all newcomers, at any number of peers), so a fresh key brings no work of its own. The work of connecting (score lookups, our subscription announce) is charged to the new peer; the work of leaving (mesh and fanout removal, one score snapshot, the retained heap, its own asks and wait places, its promises, voided at once by a per-peer mark, its chain requests ended and the retries they cause, its waiting messages moved) is charged to the leaving peer itself, and depends only on its own state plus one scan of the author check's outstanding requests (at most the peers connected x `MaxCertWants`, 12,580 entries at 1,258 peers). A key that leaves in debt or with messages waiting is kept with its debt, for `RetainScore` (10 min) when scoring is on and `WorkBurst` (2 s) when it is off, counted from its departure, at most `MaxRetained` keys, the oldest departure dropped first when another such key leaves; work done for a key after it left (a late verdict of its departed messages) is charged to its entry. A departed key with nothing kept gets an entry from that work, counted from that moment, but it never pushes out another key's debt: when `MaxRetained` keys are kept, that work goes unpaid, at most the verdicts of the departed share's messages. A returning key gets back its debt alone (each bucket's level if below zero) plus the newcomer pool's grant, its refill counted from now; a key that left without debt returns as a fresh key. kolo-swarm bounds reconnects in its own right: at most `MaxHandshakes` (16) hellos in progress, each for at most 2 s, `MaxHandshakesPerIP` (4) from one address, and a key we dial backs off from `RetryMin` (1 s) up to `RetryMax` (10 min) after a lost connection (kolo-swarm `docs/limits.md`).
- `ServeConn` closes the connection of a peer we do not serve because the application's `MaxPeers` is reached (`ErrLimit`), and of one we stopped serving for its score (graylisted) or for a stall, so it does not hold a place with gossip off; the swarm may connect again after its backoff, which `Allow` decides. A peer that ends its own streams keeps the connection, which may carry other protocols.
- An accepted message within one heartbeat of either edge of the time window is delivered but not forwarded, so the next hop never refuses it for its time.

| `PrunePeers` | 16 records | Dials per PRUNE entry | Implementations' value (Overlay and gossip); records for our own key, connected keys and repeats are left out |
| `OpDial` | 162,000 units (1.62 ms) per record queued for a dial | CPU of the handshakes a PRUNE makes us run | The slowest of three runs of `BenchmarkPXDial`: one authenticated kolo-secure dial on loopback, both sides of the handshake. Charged to the PRUNE sender's reader bucket for each record the driver queues for a dial (`Sink.Dial` returns how many it took); a record dropped because `PXPending` records wait already, or because there is no `Config.Swarm`, costs nothing. So one peer pays for at most about 68 records a second (`PeerWork` / `OpDial`) beyond what its 2 s burst holds |
| `PXPending` | 128 records waiting | Memory | Implementations' value; past it a record is dropped (`Stats.PXDropped`) |
| `PXDialers` | 8 | Dials at once | Implementations' value |
| `PXTimeout` | 30 s | A dial that hangs | Implementations' value |

Each record is dialed with `Config.Swarm.Connect` for the PRUNE's topic, with the IPv4 address of the PRUNE's sender as the record's source (zero when the sender is relayed, so the swarm dials only global addresses); any error skips the record, without a retry, and the swarm's own limits for `Connect` apply. Without `Config.Swarm` nothing is dialed. Records are unsigned (key, IPv4 address, port); the dial is authenticated by kolo-secure, so a wrong address costs one failed dial. A peer's listening port is kept only when the peer holds an outbound place (`ServeConn`'s `outbound` argument) and we opened its connection from a socket other than our swarm's, so we dialed that address; otherwise the port is 0 and the peer is never offered. `Stats.PXDials`, `PXConnected`, `PXDropped` and `RouterStats.PXAccepted` count them.

## Honest headroom

An honest mesh peer of a node, at the design rate of 1,666 messages of 1 KiB a second: one message in D (6) first from it, a copy of each of the others, one IDONTWANT frame per message (none joined), as `TestHonestHeadroomMeasured` and `TestHonestHeadroomMeasuredAt` measure it. The use does not depend on the number of peers; the share does, and with the split of `LockWork` (Overlay and gossip) it stays far above the use from 64 to 1,258 peers. With D = 6 mesh peers in every topic, each message in one topic, the mesh peers' rate is the same at every peer count of the table:

| Peers | Topics | Lock rate of a mesh peer | Honest use | Headroom | Reader rate | Reader use | Headroom |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 1 | 4,557,291 units/s | 238,016 | 19.1x | 11,032,276 | 995,435 | 11.1x |
| 1,000 | 1 | 4,191,666 | 238,016 | 17.6x | 11,032,276 | 995,435 | 11.1x |
| 1,258 | 1 | 4,186,539 | 238,016 | 17.6x | 11,032,276 | 995,435 | 11.1x |
| 1,000 | 50, all in the mesh, traffic in one | 4,191,666 | 1,272,047 | 3.3x | 11,032,276 | 995,435 | 11.1x |
| 1,258 | 50, all in the mesh, traffic in one | 4,186,539 | 1,272,047 | 3.3x | 11,032,276 | 995,435 | 11.1x |

At 50 topics the use is 5.3 times the one-topic use because a score is computed for each of the topics a peer is in on every frame, charged by its topics (4 + 2 per topic lookups); the table is the worst case, a peer in all 50 meshes, and the rates are of the last heartbeat's snapshot (a node with no mesh place yet shares equally, 50,000 units/s at 1,000 peers, until its first heartbeat 100 ms after start). A peer that alone brings every message first uses 773,358 lock units a second in one topic (0.18 of its rate at 64 and 1,258 peers) and 2,079,502 in 50 topics (0.50); its equal share before this split was 781,250 at 64 peers and 50,000 at 1,000. For a connection outside the mesh the rate is `LockWork` / 2P: 390,625 units/s at 64 peers, 25,000 at 1,000 and 19,873 at 1,258. One IHAVE of 5,000 ids we have already seen costs 40,026 lock units and 30,012 reader units (`TestIHaveOfSeenIDsCost`), so a peer outside the mesh that sends one full IHAVE every heartbeat is within its rate up to (`LockWork` / 2) / 40,026 = 625 such peers in one busy topic, and waits about 40 percent of the time at 1,000 (both DERIVED): that only delays its own repair, costs it no score, and the gossip target cap above keeps it from being asked for so many. Hostile peers outside the mesh get `LockWork` / 2P each and at most half together, so the mesh peers' rates do not depend on what they do.

Topics shared with one peer. The use grows about linearly with the topics a peer shares with us, so CPU headroom, not a count, bounds how many topics may be shared with one peer at the design rate. `TestHeadroomSharedTopics` (256 peers, all in the same topics we joined, six of them in every mesh, all the traffic in one topic, 3,332 messages in 2 s):

| Topics shared | Lock rate of a mesh peer | Honest use | Headroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 4,264,322 units/s | 1,273,049 | 3.35x |
| 100 | 4,264,322 | 2,328,182 | 1.83x |
| 200 | 4,264,322 | 4,438,449 | 0.96x |

At 200 shared topics an honest mesh peer starts to wait at the design rate; the crossover is near 190 topics (DERIVED, about linear between the rows). The test fails under 3x at 50 and 1.5x at 100. A larger `LockWork` moves the crossover in proportion; scoring a peer once between heartbeats instead of on every frame would remove the growth and is not done (Known limits).

`TestHonestHeadroom` checks that no mesh peer waits, every message is delivered and no score drops, at the design rate with bursts of twice the rate for 2 s and heartbeats up to 300 ms late or early, and at twice the design rate held for 8 s, at 64 peers. `TestHonestHeadroomAt` checks the same from 64 to 1,258 peers in one topic, at 1,000 peers in 50 topics with all the traffic in one, and with it spread over all 50. `TestHonestCatchUp` checks a catch-up of `MaxCacheMessages` ids announced by six peers: each id asked of at most two at once, every message fetched, no peer waits and no promise breaks. `TestNewPeerCatchUp` checks a peer that has just connected: it answers the 1,666 ids we ask of it within 7 ms of waiting, with what the newcomer pool gave it. The headroom above is for mesh peers, whose copies are mostly duplicates.

The application validator's CPU is the application's, outside `LockWork` and `PeerWork`: its time is not charged to anyone, as deployed implementations do not charge it either and bound validation with queues and throttles. Validators are bounded by the shares (16 messages and 1 MiB per sender), the fair share and the open tier, the departed share, the global bounds (`MaxValidating`, `MaxValidatingBytes`) and `ValidateTimeout`, and a validation the router drops is canceled. The rate at which a sender can make the validator run is bounded by its reader bucket, since each new message costs at least a signature check, and by its share, which holds at most 16 of its messages at once.

So one attacking connection outside the mesh takes at most about 8.8 ms of the router lock a second at 64 peers (the last row, the largest) and, with a rate of 19,873 units a second, 1/20 of that at 1,258 peers (DERIVED); a connection in the mesh also gets its mesh and traffic quarters; all connections outside the mesh together take at most half of `LockWork`, 250 ms a second, at any number of peers, and every connection together at most `LockWork`, 500 ms a second, plus the newcomer pool, one share (7.8 ms a second, whatever the number of peers), plus the buckets' capacities once. A connection's share changes only at a heartbeat for the mesh and traffic parts, and the rates always sum to at most the budget. Keys that come and go get nothing more: each key pays its own connect and leave work, a new key starts with what the pool gives, and a key that left in debt brings its debt back. What a key that leaves in debt and never returns leaves unpaid depends on its own state, which it paid for while connected, plus a constant: the leave walks the peers connected and the topics' meshes and fanouts, scans the author check's outstanding requests once (at most the peers connected x `MaxCertWants` entries, one lookup each), and adds one score snapshot (with the application score once) and O(log `MaxRetained`) for the retained heap. `TestLeaveCost` measures 5,034 units charged to the leaving peer at 1,258 peers and 50 topics, plus the scan of at most 12,580 entries (25,160 units, DERIVED), under one transport handshake (162,000 units, 1.62 ms for both sides, `BenchmarkPXDial`): `BenchmarkLeave` takes 8.6 us of the lock at those sizes, so a key that leaves cheaply cannot be made to leave and return faster than handshakes allow. On the readers, all connections together take at most `ReaderWork`, about 7.06 cores, whatever their number, and one connection at most `PeerWork`, 110 ms a second charged; signature checks (24.8 us measured for 30 us charged, 550 us for a 1 MiB message charged 1.15 ms), id hashing (3.1 GB/s measured, charged as 2.1 GB/s), decoding (1.7 GB/s measured, charged as 1.6 GB/s, plus one unit per list entry) and peer exchange dials (`OpDial`, the slowest measured run) are charged at or above their cost. Before, `PeerWork` was per connection, so 64 attacking connections took 6.3 cores and 1,000 took 110. Application code under the lock is charged by its measured time: a slow roster or open mode slows the peers that make us call it. The validator's CPU is outside these budgets (Honest headroom).

Other costs of many peers and topics, measured at 1,258 peers and 50 topics (`BenchmarkHeartbeat`, `BenchmarkJoinMoves`, slowest of three, at a load average of about 3): a heartbeat with no messages takes 0.08 ms of the lock, and `Join` plus `Leave` of one topic, which moves every peer's subscription between its lists and sends one frame to every peer, 0.53 ms; node work, not charged to a peer.

Memory: one ignored-id set of 8,333 x 36 bytes; asks of about 110 bytes each with their map entry (an id and up to six peer references, four asked and two waiting), each holding an asked peer after every heartbeat, so at most P x 1,666 for P peers (106,624, about 12 MB, at 64), and within a heartbeat at most one per index entry; the per-peer index of the ids a peer is asked for or waits for, about 50 bytes an entry, at most 2 x 1,666 per peer (about 11 MB for 64 peers); IWANT answer counts of about 50 bytes each, at most `MaxCacheMessages` (8,333) per peer, cleared every `HistoryLength` heartbeats and dropped when the peer leaves (about 27 MB for 64 peers); 5,000 IDONTWANT ids per peer; each peer's other-topics list (at most `MaxPeerTopics` x 32 bytes, 33 KB); each peer's delivery records, which list up to every connected peer for each message kept (40 KB at 1,258 peers), within the messages alive; each peer's validation share (16 messages, 1 MiB) and the departed share within the global bounds (65 MiB), which also hold the validations dropped while their validator runs; validators past their timeout within their senders' shares; at most `MaxRetained` (1,024) retained scores and 1,024 keys kept with a debt; at most `PXPending` (128) peer exchange records waiting; the chain cache within 2 x `MaxChains` (8,192) results, holding objects only of first checks. The per-peer tables above, with the 1 MiB peer queue, come to about 2.0 MB for one hostile peer that fills every one of them (DERIVED from the sizes above; the IDONTWANT table's map overhead is UNVERIFIED), outside the global validation bounds: 128 MB at 64 hostile peers and about 2.5 GB if all 1,258 places of kolo-swarm's default budget are hostile. Gossip leaves the number of connections to the connection layer; a node-wide pool for the queue bytes and the IWANT counts is a follow-up, not part of this release. The honest idle cost of a peer is UNVERIFIED.

## Known limits

Found in review and not fixed in this release:

- The per-frame score cost grows with the topics a peer shares with us (Honest headroom).
- A gossip heartbeat scans every peer and every gossip window once per topic, node work that is not charged to a peer. `BenchmarkHeartbeat` measures it with no messages only; at the design rate and at hundreds of topics it is not measured.
- A peer keeps its subscriptions as 32-byte topic ids and its backoffs in per-topic tables: 50 bytes per peer and shared topic for a peer that only subscribes, 124 for one that grafts every topic (measured with 200 peers over 5 heartbeats in a throwaway test, not kept), 7.8 MB at 1,258 hostile peers sharing 50 topics (DERIVED). Topic indices would cut it.
- A mesh peer that echoes back the messages we forwarded to it earns the same delivery credit as an honest one. One credit per message and peer is the most it earns, so a silent mesh peer gets no traffic share and a relaying one at most one credit per message.
- When a delivery record is full after peers left, a copy from an unrecorded peer is credited every time it arrives (`message.go`, the append of `rec.peers`).
- `ReaderWork` defaults to 64 x `PeerWork`, which a very large `PeerWork` can push past 2^50 and be refused as an invalid `ReaderWork` the caller never set.
- The simulator does not check that a peer's other-topics list stays within `MaxPeerTopics`, its honest nodes dial without regard to topics (kolo-swarm dials per topic, so its per-topic degree is higher), and its M1 rule is 128 KiB x rooms, not 50 times the one-topic value of the same run set.
- In the first 0.2 to 0.3 s, when every key is new to every other, honest mesh peers wait for the newcomer pool at 256 places (docs/design.md, Scale). The design's rule is no honest mesh wait after the warm-up; whether to size the pool for a node's simultaneous first connections is the owner's decision.
- No test runs an old node against a new one; the mixed-version rule in the changelog rests on reading the code.
- The node-wide pool for queue bytes and IWANT counts is not part of this release.

## Not limits

Topic size, the number of authors, message rate and history retention are not limits of the library. They belong to the application and to the pins.
